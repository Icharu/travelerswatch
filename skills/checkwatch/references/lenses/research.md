# Lens: Research

For academic or applied research: a thesis, a paper, a study, a grant project.

## How the sections read in this field

| Section | Ask it as |
|---|---|
| Context | Discipline and subfield, the gap in the literature, **referenced works** (key papers, datasets, prior studies), advisor/lab/funder |
| Constraints | Funding period, ethics approval, data access and licences, equipment, fieldwork seasons, word or page limits, submission deadlines |
| Problem | Research question(s) and, if any, hypotheses; final objective = the output (paper, thesis chapter, dataset) and what it must show |
| Tools | Study design, sampling, instruments, statistical or computational methods, software, reference manager |

## Default criteria (keep, drop, or add)

1. **Question is answerable** — the research question can be answered with the stated design and data.
2. **Gap is grounded** — the context cites the works that show the gap.
3. **Method matches question** — design and analysis fit the question type (descriptive, causal, exploratory).
4. **Reproducible** — data, code, and parameters are recorded well enough for someone else to repeat the analysis.
5. **Ethics and permissions are in place** before data collection.
6. **Claims stay within the evidence** — conclusions do not exceed what the analysis supports.

## Drift signals

- The research question is reworded to fit results already obtained.
- New variables or analyses appear without a reason tied to the question (fishing).
- Referenced works stop being cited in the methods.
- A method changes because a tool was convenient, not because the question required it.

## Typical tools

R, Python, QGIS, SPSS, Stata, NVivo; Zotero, Mendeley; preregistration platforms (OSF);
repositories such as Zenodo for data and code.
