# Lens: Project Management (default)

For project managers steering a team, a client engagement, or any time-bound initiative.
Criteria below are written for this skill; they are not quoted from any published standard.

## How the sections read in this field

| Section | Ask it as |
|---|---|
| Context | Sponsor and stakeholders, organization, project phase, prior projects or business case it builds on |
| Constraints | Schedule, budget, team capacity, contractual terms, organizational policies, dependencies on other teams |
| Problem | The business or user problem today; the deliverable and the acceptance condition that closes the project |
| Tools | Tracking (board, Gantt), communication, documentation, delivery methods (Scrum, Kanban, waterfall, hybrid) |

## Default criteria (keep, drop, or add)

1. **Scope is written down** — a scope statement or equivalent exists and the sponsor approved it.
2. **Objective is measurable** — the final objective has a metric or acceptance condition and a date.
3. **Stakeholders are known** — who decides, who is consulted, who is informed.
4. **Plan fits constraints** — the schedule and budget are consistent with team capacity.
5. **Risks are named** — the top risks have an owner and a response.
6. **Changes are controlled** — scope changes are proposed, approved, and logged (here: `/fixwatch`).
7. **Progress is visible** — current status can be read from the tracking tool without asking anyone.

## Drift signals

- Work appears that no stakeholder asked for (gold-plating).
- A new deliverable is discussed as if already approved.
- Deadlines move without a logged decision.
- The team talks about a tool or method change that is not in *Tools*.
- The final objective is quietly reworded in conversation.

## Typical tools

GitHub Projects, Jira, Linear, OpenProject, Taiga, Plane, Trello; Gantt or roadmap tools;
shared documents for scope and decisions; Scrum, Kanban, or stage-gate processes.

## Named external standards users often pick here

PMI's PMBOK® Guide, ISO 21500 / 21502, PRINCE2®, the Scrum Guide, Kanban Method.
Evaluate from general knowledge of these frameworks; reference sections by name only.
