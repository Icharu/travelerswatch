# The Traveler's Watch v1.2.3

**Released:** 2026-11-26 · **DOI:** [10.5281/zenodo.24543183](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24543183) (all versions) · **Full changelog:** [v1.2.2...v1.2.3](https://github.com/Icharu/travelerswatch/compare/v1.2.2...v1.2.3)

A **documentation release**. The skills, hook, logbook format, and license are the same as in
[v1.2.0](https://github.com/Icharu/travelerswatch/blob/v1.2.3/docs/releases/v1.2.0.md), so nothing changes in how TTW behaves. What changes is how the documentation
speaks: every claim is now a design intent, a mechanism you can check in a file, or a measured
result, and the reader can tell which.

## Correction: who reviewed the 97 publications

Earlier versions said Koshman & Zubkova (2026) reviewed 97 publications on AI in project management
and found three unresolved limitations. Their paper reports that review; it is not theirs:

> "According to D. Adamantiadou & L. Tsironis (2025) … A review of 97 publications by the authors
> found that AI methods improved forecast accuracy, automated decision-making, and optimised project
> task management during the planning, implementation, and closure stages. However, the authors
> pointed to several unresolved limitations…"

The review is **Adamantiadou & Tsironis (2025)**, a PRISMA review of 97 peer-reviewed studies
(2011–2024). Koshman & Zubkova's own review covers 21 publications (2021–2025). TTW's design is
unchanged, because the limitations are the same; the attribution, and what was read, are now stated
plainly:

| Source | What was read |
|---|---|
| Koshman & Zubkova (2026) | Full text |
| Adamantiadou & Tsironis (2025) | Koshman & Zubkova's summary, and the abstract and bibliographic metadata of its DOI record; full text not read |

This also corrects the [v1.2.0 release notes](https://github.com/Icharu/travelerswatch/blob/v1.2.3/docs/releases/v1.2.0.md), which are archived on Zenodo and left as
published.

## Other fixes

- **Benchmark timing:** the checkpoint on real evidence came **5 days** after the bearing was set,
  not 15.
- **Literature window:** "five years of research (2021–2026)" was Koshman & Zubkova's search window.
  TTW draws on literature published 2020–2026.
- **AI-use disclosure** ([GOVERNANCE.md](https://github.com/Icharu/travelerswatch/blob/v1.2.3/GOVERNANCE.md)):
  every commit through v1.2.3 was written with Claude Code, except merges and two GitHub web edits,
  with the command to list them.

## Clearer about what TTW does, and what it costs

- **Rules are described as rules.** "TTW refuses" is now "the skills are written to refuse … tested
  by evals, not enforced by code", in line with [ADR 0004](https://github.com/Icharu/travelerswatch/blob/v1.2.3/docs/adr/0004-human-in-the-loop.md).
- **A design premise, not a finding.** The README no longer says AI-led projects are inefficient;
  no source measured that. It states TTW's premise as the maintainer's reading of the review.
- **New README section: What it costs and where it fits.** Prerequisites, measured per-prompt cost
  (hook median 6–11 ms; ≈ 120–650 tokens injected, capped at 4,000 characters), intake effort
  (about 4 to 11 rounds in the v1.0.0 benchmark), and where TTW may work less well. All figures come
  from the [existing benchmark](https://github.com/Icharu/travelerswatch/blob/v1.2.3/docs/benchmarks/2026-11-25-best-vs-worst.md).
- **Network use, stated precisely.** The hook makes no network calls, and the README gives a
  command to check. The `/checkwatch` evidence scan may list open issues and pull requests through
  your forge CLI (for example `gh`) if it is installed; that is now disclosed.

## Translations

All six translations (pt-BR, es, zh-Hans, zh-Hant, ja, ar) carry the same changes. Spanish now uses
neutral international Spanish. [CONTRIBUTING.md](https://github.com/Icharu/travelerswatch/blob/v1.2.3/CONTRIBUTING.md#translations)
adds a rule to keep each claim's type across languages, and a glossary of claim-bearing terms.
Translations are machine-assisted; native-speaker reviews are welcome.
